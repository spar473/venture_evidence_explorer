# Venture Outlook

Venture Outlook is VEE's rookie-style preview. Open `?view=rookie` from the analytics header. A shareable case uses `?view=rookie&venture=breast-mri`. The page uses the Company Profile and Scenario Lab mock-ups as its design reference: choose the subject, inspect dated peer paths, open the source for any chart mark and adjust the future scenario inputs.

## What is available

- Eight early New Zealand venture or historical entry profiles, two in each of the four research categories.
- A read-only research catalogue with 40 ventures and 149 source-linked milestone rows from the supplied 40-venture report. Programme announcements establish the subject snapshots at the published dates. They do not establish all activity on those dates or any current venture status.
- Three edited analogues per subject. Each comparison describes the relevant similarity and its limit. Some are later-stage pathway analogues. Chart time zero is a named milestone on each peer timeline. Original event dates and conservative elapsed time ranges remain visible. Playback uses elapsed time, rather than asserting historical source availability at a common calendar cutoff.
- A 36-month conditional model with a one- or two-step path. Advance and stall rates are editable quarterly assumptions. A stalled path is absorbing in this model. A path can advance only once each quarter. Pending mass stays in its current state. The probabilities are computed directly by propagating mass across the states, and the assumed inputs are shown next to the chart. The same input probabilities apply at each step when two steps are selected. The outcome curves describe the selected assumptions and have not been fitted to venture outcomes.

The report-derived rows are research evidence for editorial inspection. The public preview does not confer human confirmation, formal same-stage eligibility, or statistical relevance on them. The formal Time Machine track uses separate availability and review rules. A start-year or fiscal reporting interval can span multiple possible event dates. Overlapping intervals can show a zero lower elapsed bound. The relative chart only displays a follow-up when its upper elapsed bound is within the selected window.

## Funding & outcomes

Open `?view=evidence` to search all 40 ventures and browse the 149 observations. A peer card links directly to `?view=evidence&case=…`. Filter by category, origin, venture and type. Editorial metadata distinguishes equity, debt, programme support, public-market transactions, acquisitions, setbacks and other milestones. Known amounts retain their source currency and scope; the ledger has no cross-currency totals. Redwood's conditional debt, Kami's investor realisation and Linca's separately attributed first close illustrate why event status and entity scope are first-class fields in the display.

The catalogue is a selected public-evidence layer. Its event types do not expand the reviewed Time Machine outcome census and the absence of a displayed funding record does not establish that a venture was unfunded.

## Working ML baselines

- **Text retrieval.** Fit smoothed inverse-document-frequency weights on the 40-venture catalogue's starting-record descriptions and editorial scope labels, with log-scaled term frequency and cosine similarity. Restrict results to the selected category. Either the NZ subject's opening facts or an analyst's query can drive search. Show the highest-contributing shared terms. A researcher can plot one additional hit at its first dossier record or choose any remaining case manually. This discovery layer uses the first dossier row, whose historical public-availability status remains unverified. It uses no later event text, performance targets or human relevance labels.
- **Evidence tagging.** A multinomial naive Bayes model fits the 149 catalogue summaries and four grouped editorial labels. A researcher can paste a public excerpt into the ledger and review a proposed type. Text stays in the browser and a suggestion changes no records. The classifier abstains when the snippet has fewer than two distinct in-vocabulary terms.
- **Diagnostic.** Leaving one entire venture out at a time, the model matched 110 of 149 editorial labels (73.8%, with abstentions counted as misses), versus 80 of 149 for the training-fold majority baseline. Macro-F1 was 0.62. It abstained on 11 records and correctly tagged only one of 15 setback observations. The source summaries share an editorial style and the tags have not been independently labelled. The diagnostic measures performance on this corpus and does not establish performance on raw sources. The suggestion is for review only.

Reproduce the diagnostic from `frontend/` with `node --experimental-strip-types test/venture-ml.mjs`. The script also verifies corpus counts, unknown-text abstention and the Linca financing scope. The browser suite tests ranked search, adding a fourth chart lane, the evidence deep link, funding filters, the classifier UI and responsive layout.

## Where machine learning adds value first

**Learn to rank relevant precedents.** The supervised target is relevance to a specific question, such as whether a case helps a founder prepare for a provider evaluation. Ask reviewers to grade eligible candidate pairs from 0 to 3, using the commercialisation question and only their entry evidence. Capture modality or offering match, buyer and channel match, stage, local transferability and missingness as separate features. Keep the historical outcome hidden during grading so later successes do not become a shortcut to relevance. Split by subject and linked venture families. Evaluate the current rules and Gower similarity first. Start with an interpretable pairwise logistic ranker and try LambdaMART after enough reviewed queries exist. Report nDCG@3, eligibility coverage and the usefulness of a justified abstention. The learned ranker orders candidates inside the hard evidence and chronology checks. [LambdaMART overview](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/from-ranknet-to-lambdarank-to-lambdamart-an-overview/) · [Graded relevance evaluation](https://doi.org/10.1145/582415.582418).

**Learn to classify evidence.** A separate supervised classifier can suggest whether a passage documents an announced pilot, completed deployment, receipt of funding, or another event. Keep its labels and evaluation separate from relevance grades. Start with the evidence labels already proposed in the 40-venture report. This gives VEE a measurable ML workflow even while longitudinal outcome data are being collected.

**Learn milestone probabilities when follow-up supports it.** The independent supervised target is whether a named outcome occurred by a given horizon. A ranker's relevance score and an evidence classifier's confidence cannot substitute for that target. The following benchmarks belong to that separate experiment.

## Models to develop and test

1. **Reference-class baseline.** Freeze the population and initial milestone. Compute cumulative incidence of a first qualifying institutional equity round by 12, 24 and 36 months. Use Aalen–Johansen with positively verified closure as a competing event where the follow-up supports it. Track last reliable follow-up and analyse possible informative observation loss. The adjacent outcome in education might be renewal, while industrial cases may warrant first facility commissioning. Predefine and estimate those separately.
2. **Regularised discrete-time model.** Build dated features such as offering, buyer, documented prototype or named pilot, and periods at risk. A person-period multinomial model gives probabilities for advance, closure and remaining at risk. Compare its cumulative predictions with the cohort baseline. Estimate shared effects before expanding to separate sector estimates. When transition data exist, the same state model can simulate pilot → paid adoption → renewal instead of an all-purpose success score.
3. **Tabular AI benchmarks.** Evaluate [CatBoost](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2018/hash/14491b756b3a51daac41c24863285549-Abstract.html) on exactly the same period rows and splits for possible nonlinear interactions. Test [TabPFN](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-024-08328-6) as a fixed-horizon benchmark where outcome observability supports the comparison. Its tabular classification interface does not resolve censoring or selection bias. A [competing-risk survival forest](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24728979/) is a further time-to-event benchmark once outcome volume supports it. Use an LLM for claim extraction and a source-grounded explanation of the selected comparisons. Keep test outcomes, post-cutoff evidence and company identifiers out of model features.
4. **Release only after forward validation.** Audit complete programme-entry cohorts first, beginning with the public Velocity 2020 and 2023 finalists. Keep the 40 hand-picked examples for comparative inspection. In each forward split, train on older evidence and score later NZ cohorts at their original cutoffs. Group linked ventures together. Compare horizon Brier scores, calibration, coverage and uncertainty, including robustness by geography, vintage and missing follow-up. Show a forecast alongside observed paths only where it improves on the baseline and has acceptable calibration for the intended use. [Rolling-origin evaluation](https://otexts.com/fpp3/tscv.html) · [Proper scoring rules](https://stat.uw.edu/research/tech-reports/strictly-proper-scoring-rules-prediction-and-estimation).

A company probability, a historical peer's known outcome and a share calculated from user-supplied assumptions represent three different quantities. In a later release, use separate traces and legends so an analyst can see which quantity is being shown.
